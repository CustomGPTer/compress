Privacy policy

Last updated 16 November 2026. This policy covers the Compress app for Android (package uk.jj.compress) and this website. Compress is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom.

The short version

  • Your videos are read and compressed on your phone. They are never uploaded, to the developer or to anyone else.
  • Compress has no accounts and no servers of its own. The developer receives nothing from your phone.
  • There is no advertising and no analytics in the app.
  • The one thing the app uses the internet for is to ask GitHub whether a newer version is out. The detail is below.

What stays on your phone

Compress reads a video where it is kept and saves the smaller copy to the Movies/Compress folder, or to another folder you choose in Settings, where your gallery and other apps can see it. GIFs are saved to Pictures/Compress and sound files to Music/Compress. While a video is being compressed, the unfinished copy is held in the app's own working folder and removed afterwards.

The app keeps the following in its private storage: your settings; the queue of videos waiting or just finished; a note of which videos have already been compressed, so that they can be marked and filtered; a list of the originals it has moved to Android's bin at your request; and, only if you switch it on, a tally of the space saved with the names of the videos. A video leaves your phone only when you choose to send it somewhere yourself, with Share.

GitHub also receives your device's internet address, as any online service does, and the name of the app. It handles this under the GitHub Privacy Statement. The developer of Compress receives none of it.

Compressing needs no internet connection at all. With the daily check switched off, the app contacts nothing.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • Copies in Movies/Compress stay until you delete them from your gallery.
  • Originals that you asked Compress to replace are held in Android's bin for 30 days and then deleted by Android. Until then you can put them back, or delete them at once, under Settings, then Bin.
  • The space-saved tally is off unless you turn it on, and Clear wipes it.
  • Clearing the app's storage or uninstalling Compress deletes everything it holds. Videos you saved stay on your phone.
  • If your phone's own backup is switched on, Android may include Compress's settings in it. The queue, the tally and the other lists are left out. That backup is yours and is managed in your phone's settings.

Your rights

UK and EU data protection law gives you rights over personal data that an organisation holds about you, including the right to see it, correct it and have it deleted.

The developer of Compress holds no personal data about you: there is no account, no server and no log of who uses the app. Your videos are on your own phone, where you control them directly using the steps above. If you need help, use the contact link below. You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Security

The app's own data is held in private storage that other apps cannot read. Videos you save are ordinary files in your phone's shared storage, as they were before.

The app's connection to GitHub uses HTTPS.

This website

This site sets no cookies, runs no analytics and loads nothing from other websites. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub, as the host, receives the internet address of visitors and handles it under the GitHub Privacy Statement. The app file is downloaded from GitHub as well, on the same terms.

Children

Compress is a general-purpose video tool. It is not directed at children and its developer does not knowingly collect information from anyone.

Changes

If the app changes in a way that affects this policy, this page will be updated and the date at the top changed.
